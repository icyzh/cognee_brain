## Context
This supersedes ADR-003, which put the payments ledger on MongoDB. In February the TCK-131 incident caused a double refund: two concurrent captures and a refund on the same payment each read the balance, and both refunds were written. A refund updates the payment record and writes two ledger entries, and MongoDB could not give us multi-document ACID guarantees across those ledger entries at our write pattern without a lot of fragile application code. A ledger that can double-spend is not acceptable.

## Decision
Move svc-ledger and svc-payments storage to Postgres. Every money movement (payment update plus its ledger entries) is written in one serializable transaction, with a database constraint that balances cannot go negative. Priya Sharma owns the migration (TCK-142). Marco Rossi, who wrote ADR-003, agreed.

## Alternatives considered
- Stay on MongoDB and use its multi-document transactions: tested during the incident review, but performance and retry behaviour under our contention were poor.
- DynamoDB: scales well but offers weaker transactional guarantees and no relational queries for reconciliation.

## Consequences
Correctness of money movement is enforced by the database. The migration needs a short write freeze, planned for a low-traffic window. svc-reports' replica (ADR-004) becomes a native Postgres replica.
