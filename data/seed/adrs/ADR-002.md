## Context
svc-payments calls svc-notify synchronously after every capture and refund to send receipts. When svc-notify is slow, payment requests slow down with it, and when it is down, receipts are silently lost. Notifications should never be able to affect the payment path.

## Decision
svc-payments publishes payment events (authorised, captured, refunded) to an SQS queue after the payment is committed. svc-notify consumes the queue and retries with backoff; failed messages go to a dead-letter queue that Mei Chen's team monitors.

## Alternatives considered
- Keep the synchronous call with a shorter timeout: still couples the two services and still drops receipts.
- Kafka: gives replay and ordering, but nobody on the team runs it today and our volume does not need it. SQS is managed and we already use AWS.

## Consequences
Payment latency is decoupled from notifications. Delivery is at-least-once, so svc-notify must deduplicate on event id. Receipts may arrive a few seconds after the payment.
